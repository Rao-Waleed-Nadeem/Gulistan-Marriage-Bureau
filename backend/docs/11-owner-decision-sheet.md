## Decision 1: Authentication Method for Candidates & Staff
* **Tracking ID:** [`OPEN-001`](09-open-decisions.md#91-initial-decision-log)
* **Question:** How should users log into their accounts on Gulistan Marriage Bureau?

### Context
In Pakistan, marriage bureau candidates and their families have diverse digital habits. Matchmakers and administrators use desktop laptops daily, whereas candidate profiles are often viewed or managed by parents (fathers/mothers) on mobile phones who may rarely check email.

### Options
* **Option A — Email and Password (Standard Web)**
  * *How it works:* Users register and log in with their email address and a personal password.
  * *Pros:* Zero cost; standard across all web hosting providers; straightforward account recovery via email reset.
  * *Cons:* Many Pakistani family elders do not remember passwords or actively monitor email inboxes.
* **Option B — Magic Link via Email (Passwordless)**
  * *How it works:* User enters email; a one-click sign-in link is sent to their inbox. No password needed.
  * *Pros:* Highly secure; no forgotten passwords.
  * *Cons:* Requires opening email on the same device each time; unfamiliar to some non-technical users.
* **Option C — Phone / SMS OTP (Mobile First)**
  * *How it works:* User receives a 4- or 6-digit code via SMS on their Pakistani mobile number (`+92 3XX XXXXXXX`).
  * *Pros:* Culturally the most natural, accessible method for candidates and parents across Pakistan.
  * *Cons:* SMS gateways (e.g. Twilio or local Pakistani telco aggregators) charge per SMS ($0.03–$0.05 per text), requiring a billing balance.
* **Option D (Recommended Hybrid) — Staff uses Email/Password; Candidates/Families use Magic Link or WhatsApp Verified Link**
  * *How it works:* Staff (Nadeem and Admin) use secure Email + Password with high-entropy requirements. Family clients access their private view via secure, single-use verified links generated by the matchmaker or delivered via email/WhatsApp.
  * *Pros:* Zero SMS infrastructure cost to start; maximum security for staff administrative powers.

## Decision 2: Staff Operational Scope & Access Hierarchy
* **Tracking ID:** [`OPEN-002`](09-open-decisions.md#91-initial-decision-log)
* **Question:** Is Mr. Nadeem Ahmed the sole matchmaker initially, or should the database support multiple independent matchmakers with segregated candidate lists?

### Options
* **Option A (Recommended for Initial Launch) — Single Lead Matchmaker Model**
  * *How it works:* Nadeem Ahmed is the authoritative lead matchmaker and has access to all active candidates, follow-ups, and partner ledger transactions. An Administrator account exists for technical and governance oversight.
  * *Consequences:* Simpler database security rules (RLS); faster development; matches current operations.
* **Option B — Multi-Agent Matchmaker Network**
  * *How it works:* The bureau employs multiple staff matchmakers. Each candidate is assigned to a specific matchmaker. Staff only see their assigned candidates, while Nadeem/Admin sees all.
  * *Consequences:* Requires complex row-level permission policies, assignment re-allocation logic, and team management dashboards.

## Decision 3: Public Candidate Registration & Intake Model
* **Tracking ID:** [`OPEN-003`](09-open-decisions.md#91-initial-decision-log)
* **Question:** Should prospective candidates or families be allowed to register directly on the public website, or must all profiles be entered by the bureau staff?

### Options
* **Option A (Recommended) — Closed Concierge Intake (Staff Entry Only)**
  * *How it works:* No public self-registration on the website. Families contact the bureau via WhatsApp, phone, or in-person walk-in. Nadeem or staff interviews the family and enters the verified profile into the 7-step wizard.
  * *Pros:* Eliminates spam, fake profiles, and malicious bots completely; preserves the high-touch, dignified luxury reputation of the bureau; ensures data completeness before entry.
  * *Cons:* Bureau staff must manually enter every candidate.
* **Option B — Public Web Intake with Mandatory Staff Review Gate**
  * *How it works:* Anyone can complete the 7-step registration wizard on the public website. However, the profile is immediately saved as `pending_review` and completely invisible to other users until staff personally verifies and approves it.
  * *Pros:* Scales candidate acquisition; allows families to type their own details at leisure.
  * *Cons:* Requires spam protection (Cloudflare Turnstile / CAPTCHA), rate limiting, and staff time filtering junk submissions.

## Decision 4: Candidate Consent & Guardian Authority
* **Tracking ID:** [`OPEN-004`](09-open-decisions.md#91-initial-decision-log)
* **Question:** When a parent or representative submits a profile, what proof of candidate consent is required before photos or details can be shared?

### Context
In Pakistani cultural practice, parents (father, mother) or elder siblings often manage matrimonial profiles on behalf of the prospective bride or groom. However, legal and ethical standards require that the individual candidate has actually authorized the search and photo disclosure.

### Options
* **Option A (Recommended) — Representative Legal Certification Checkbox**
  * *How it works:* The registration wizard records the submitter's identity (e.g. "Father", "Mother", "Self") and requires checking a formal certification: *"I certify that the candidate is aware of and has granted explicit permission for this matrimonial profile and photo evaluation."*
  * *Pros:* Clear legal boundary without creating high friction for respectful family inquiries.
* **Option B — Direct Candidate Digital Confirmation**
  * *How it works:* If a parent registers a candidate, an automated verification message is sent to the candidate's personal phone number or email requiring them to tap "Confirm".
  * *Pros:* Absolute proof of consent.
  * *Cons:* Can offend traditional parents or cause friction in conservative family workflows.
* **Option C — Staff Verbal Verification Call**
  * *How it works:* Staff calls the candidate or family directly during the `pending_review` stage to confirm mutual family agreement before marking the profile `active`.

## Decision 5: Production Hosting Budget & Infrastructure Tier
* **Tracking ID:** [`OPEN-013`](09-open-decisions.md#91-initial-decision-log)
* **Question:** What is the approved infrastructure budget for hosting the database, private photo storage, and authentication services?

### Context
The initial Software Requirements Specification (SRS) mentions exploring free tiers during early development, but free cloud database plans (such as Supabase Free) feature automated project pausing after periods of inactivity, limited storage quotas (500 MB), and strict egress caps.

### Options
* **Option A (Recommended) — Free Development Tier $\to$ Upgrade to Managed Pro ($25/mo) at Launch**
  * *How it works:* Use Supabase Free Tier for automated local and staging development. Once real client data and production domains are configured, upgrade to the $25/month Pro plan to disable project pausing, enable daily automated backups, and unlock private storage for candidate photographs.
  * *Budget:* $0/month during development; ~$25/month (~₨ 7,000/month) at launch.
* **Option B — Strict $0 Budget (Free Tier Only with Keep-Alive Workarounds)**
  * *How it works:* Rely strictly on free tiers across services.
  * *Risks:* High risk of service suspension; database sleeps if inactive; limited backup retention; manual intervention required to maintain uptime. Not recommended for production candidate records.

## Decision 6: Data Jurisdiction, Storage Region & Regulatory Review
* **Tracking ID:** [`OPEN-014`](09-open-decisions.md#91-initial-decision-log)
* **Question:** In which geographic cloud region should candidate records and private photographs be physically hosted?

### Options
* **Option A (Recommended) — Middle East (Bahrain / UAE) or Singapore Cloud Region**
  * *Details:* Host data with an enterprise cloud provider (e.g. AWS / Supabase) located in the Middle East (`me-central-1`) or Singapore (`ap-southeast-1`).
  * *Pros:* Low network latency from Pakistan (40–70 ms ping); robust international physical security standards (SOC 2, ISO 27001).
* **Option B — Europe (Frankfurt / Ireland)**
  * *Details:* Host in Frankfurt (`eu-central-1`).
  * *Pros:* Maximum global privacy protections (GDPR compliance baseline); mature server infrastructure.
  * *Cons:* Slightly higher network latency from Pakistan (120–150 ms).
